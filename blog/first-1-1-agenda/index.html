How to run your first 1:1 as a new engineering manager

Most new managers walk into their first 1:1 with a vague plan to "check in," and walk out having learned almost nothing. Not because the person across from them wasn't willing to talk — but because "how's it going" is an easy question to answer with nothing.

Start with a fixed structure, not a free-form chat

A 1:1 without structure tends to default to whatever's loudest that week — a blocked ticket, a minor annoyance — and skips the things that actually matter but are easy to avoid saying out loud. A simple three-part structure fixes most of this on its own:

  • Them first. Whatever they want to bring up, before you bring up anything of yours.
  • One forward-looking question. Something about where they want to be in three or six months, not just this week's work.
  • Your items last. Feedback, context, or asks from you go at the end, not the start — so they don't spend the whole meeting anticipating what you'll say.

Three questions that get past the status update

Instead of "how's it going," try questions that are harder to answer with one word:

  • "What's the most annoying part of your week, even if it's small?"
  • "Is there anything you're doing right now that you think shouldn't be your job?"
  • "What would make the next month easier, that's actually in my control to change?"

None of these are dramatic. That's the point — they're specific enough that a one-word answer doesn't quite fit, which is usually enough to get a real one instead.

What to skip in the first few 1:1s

Resist the urge to turn early 1:1s into performance conversations, even lightly. Trust has to exist before feedback lands the way you intend it to — in the first few weeks, the job is mostly to listen and build the muscle of a working structure, not to start correcting things yet.
